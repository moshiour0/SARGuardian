So which one is the scar?

The index says a surface stopped being snow. It cannot say what replaced it — bedrock, mud, or debris dumped onto clean snow all read identically. And the window spans 27 days, so ordinary melt is inside it.

Three causes, one signature. You need something the index does not have.

The precursor was real — and somebody else measured it

A separate study using Sentinel-1 found this slope creeping at about 0.33 mm a day from January to August 2026, speeding up at the end. About the rate human hair grows.

So the conclusion is not "nothing happened". It is: a signal existed and NISAR's feature tracking was roughly 30 to 100 times too coarse to resolve it — line of sight against line of sight, from a 1 km average to a single pixel. Both this collapse and Blatten in 2025 were caught by wave-pattern interferometry. But at the candidate that matches the published elevation, NISAR's own interferograms hold no valid pixel in any pair: the fix is phase that survives on a steep north face, not just "use phase".

The winter products are NISAR's pre-calibration BETA release and the summer ones calibrated PROVISIONAL, and nothing was released for 20 January – 17 June 2026 — the months the creep was building. Some of what this shows is the archive, not the physics.

Four things we published and withdrew

"Nothing was moving." A floor describes your instrument, never the world.

"The scar is at this coordinate." You found four candidates. We had reported one, to five decimals, because we pointed the search at it.

"Frequent passes cause false alarms." An error in our own threshold. Corrected, the false-alarm rate is zero at every revisit tested.

"180 to 360 times too insensitive." That divided a downslope bound by a line-of-sight rate, used one pixel against a slope average, and took its upper end from a candidate with no valid offsets at all.
