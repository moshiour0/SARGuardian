So which one is the scar?

That is the honest problem, and it is the one a reviewer caught us on. This index says a surface stopped being snow. It cannot say what replaced it — bedrock, mud, or debris dumped on top of clean snow all look identical. And the comparison spans 27 days, so ordinary late-summer melt is inside it too.

Three different causes, one signature. You need something the index does not have.

The precursor was real. It was measured — by somebody else.

A separate study using Europe's Sentinel-1 satellite found this slope creeping at about 0.33 mm a day from January to August 2026, speeding up in the final weeks. About the rate human hair grows.

So the honest conclusion is not "nothing was happening". It is: a signal existed, and this instrument was two orders of magnitude away from resolving it. That is a specification, not a failure — and it names the fix. Both this collapse and the 2025 Blatten collapse were caught by wave-pattern interferometry, never by the feature-tracking method whose floor you just measured.

Three things we published and had to withdraw

"Nothing was moving." There was a precursor. We could not see it. A floor describes your instrument, never the world.

"The scar is at this coordinate." You found four candidates in station 4. We had reported one, to five decimals, because we pointed the search at it.

"Frequent satellite passes cause false alarms." An error in our own threshold. Corrected, the false-alarm rate is zero at every revisit we tested.
